The core idea: Your audience is overwhelmed. When you lay out a clear 3-phase plan, you remove the biggest barrier to joining: not knowing where to start. The plan is what they are buying, not just the content.
Fits your community if: Your niche has a logical sequence people can follow. Works brilliantly when the topic feels complicated or intimidating to newcomers.
Builder advantage: Name your Skool classroom modules after each phase so the about page and the product feel like one seamless blueprint from the moment they join.
Copy template — replace the brackets with your own words
Most people fail at [niche] not because they lack talent. They fail because nobody gave them a clear plan. Here is the exact blueprint I use with every member: Phase 1: [Foundation step — what they do first] Phase 2: [Build step — what creates momentum] Phase 3: [Result step — what they now have] Inside [Community Name] you get: - [Primary resource or course] - [Live support element] - [Community or accountability element] [Price]/mo. Cancel any time. [X] builders already working the plan.
Common mistakes with this blueprint
1
Making the phases too vague. "Learn, Practice, Grow" tells nobody anything. Your phases need to describe a specific action or outcome. "Map your niche, build your first module, launch to 50 members" is a blueprint. Generic labels are not.
2
Naming 5 or 6 phases instead of 3. The power of this format is simplicity. More steps signals complexity, which is the exact problem you are trying to solve. If you have more than 3, collapse them.

The core idea: Lead with a short sprint challenge. A quick tangible win builds trust faster than any sales pitch and converts people into long-term paying members before they even think about cancelling.
Fits your community if: You can deliver a real, nameable result within 7 to 30 days. The more specific the win, the better this works.
Builder advantage: Pin the next sprint start date in your Events tab. That visible date on your about page turns general interest into "I need to join this week."
Copy template — replace the brackets with your own words
Join the [X]-Day [Result] Sprint. Free with membership. Give me [X] minutes a day for [X] days and I will walk you through exactly how to [specific outcome]. After that you get: - [Ongoing resource] - [Support element] - [Number] members sharing progress and helping each other [Price]/mo. Next sprint kicks off [date or day]. Grab your spot before it fills.
Common mistakes with this blueprint
1
Promising a result that takes longer than 30 days. "Lose 10kg in 7 days" kills credibility. "Write your first 3 emails and send to a list of 100" in 7 days is believable and specific. The win needs to be real and achievable in the timeframe you name.
2
Not naming the Day 1 win. The hook is results before you pay a cent. If you only describe the Day 7 outcome, you lose the momentum. Name something they will accomplish or feel differently about on Day 1. That is the real sell.

The core idea: Your proof does the persuading. Real-world results, verified numbers, and notable names remove the need for clever copy. You list what you have done and let that speak for itself.
Fits your community if: You have genuinely impressive credentials, measurable client results, certifications, or media recognition that can be stated as facts.

Common mistakes with this blueprint
1
Leading with credentials that do not connect to the outcome. A PhD in biochemistry does not build trust for a business coaching community. Every credential you list should make the reader think "that is exactly why I would trust them with this specific thing."
2
Writing a resume instead of a sales page. Listing 12 achievements in a row is exhausting to read. Pick your 3 most relevant and most impressive. Then move quickly into what that experience means for the member, not just for your ego.

The Crew Magnet
Community First $9 to $15/mo
Your crew
The core idea: The people in the room are the product. You are not selling a course or a curriculum. You are selling access to a specific group who share the same passion, obsession, or identity.
Fits your community if: Your niche is passion-driven and the social element is a major part of the value. Hobbies, creative fields, lifestyle niches, and enthusiast communities are ideal.
Builder advantage: Lean into the Skool leaderboard and gamification in your copy. Mentioning how members earn points and unlock content reinforces that this is an active, alive community, not a static course library.
Copy template — replace the brackets with your own words
If you are a [identity label], you just found your crew. This is not a course you watch alone. It is a room full of people who actually get it. Inside we share: - [What members post or talk about] - [Signature activity or weekly ritual] - [The thing that makes the energy here different] No beginner bashing. No gatekeeping. Just [X] people obsessed with [niche topic] helping each other get better. [Price]/mo. Less than [relatable comparison]. Come in, say hello, and feel the difference within 5 minutes.
Common mistakes with this blueprint
1
Describing a vibe without proving it exists. "Amazing supportive community" is what everyone says. Describe a specific thing that happens inside: a weekly ritual, a type of post, a recurring conversation. That makes the energy feel real and not just aspirational.
2
Forgetting to name the identity. This blueprint works because people join when they feel "these are my people." The identity label does the heavy lifting. "If you are a serious home barista who thinks about extraction pressure for fun" is a hook. "Coffee lovers" is not.

The Loaded Toolbox
Price Anchoring $29 to $119/mo

The core idea: You give every item in your toolbox a dollar value, then show the total against your actual price. The gap between what they would pay separately and what membership costs is what converts people.
Fits your community if: You have at least 3 to 4 distinct resources you could each put a price on. Courses, templates, swipe files, tools, and live calls all work. Best used at $29/mo and above.
Builder advantage: List each Skool classroom module as a separate line item with its own dollar value. The community access itself gets its own value too. Your about page becomes a genuine price comparison.
Copy template — replace the brackets with your own words
Here is everything inside [Community Name]: [Resource or Course A] — valued at $[X] [Resource or Course B] — valued at $[X] [Live calls or coaching] — valued at $[X]/mo [Bonus resource or template pack] — valued at $[X] [Community access and peer support] — priceless Total value: $[X,XXX]+ Your price: $[XX]/mo. Cancel any time. That is less than $[X] a day to get [the transformation they want]. [X] members already inside. Join them.
Common mistakes with this blueprint
1
Inflating the values so much it loses credibility. If your community costs $29/mo and you are claiming $4,997 in value, people do the maths and it feels fake. Keep the ratio believable: 10x is compelling, 100x triggers scepticism.
2
Listing things people do not actually want. A 47-page PDF workbook is not worth $297 in most people's minds. Price what people genuinely value: live access, templates they can actually use, courses with real outcomes. Padding the stack with filler kills the whole format.

The Gated Site
Exclusivity $49 to $500+/mo
Apply to enter
not everyone gets a hard hat for this site
The core idea: Membership requires qualifying, not just paying. An application process signals that the people inside have been vetted and the standard of the room is protected. Scarcity and selectivity drive desire.
Fits your community if: You serve business owners, senior professionals, or advanced practitioners who will pay a premium to be in a room with peers at the same level. Quality of membership is the selling point.
Builder advantage: Your Skool membership questions become visible social proof of the standard you hold. Frame them on your about page as a qualifier, not a form. "We ask three questions before approving" signals this is a selective build.
Copy template — replace the brackets with your own words
This community is not open to everyone. [Community Name] is built for [specific qualifier] who are serious about [outcome]. We keep numbers tight because the value here comes from who else is in the room. What approved members get: - [Premium resource one] - [Premium resource two] - Direct access to [expert, network, or inner circle] - [What makes the peer group genuinely valuable] You will be asked three questions before your spot is confirmed. We review every application personally. $[XX]/mo. [X] members. Waitlist open now. Apply below and we will be in touch within [timeframe].
Common mistakes with this blueprint
1
Claiming exclusivity without actually being exclusive. If you approve every applicant automatically, people find out quickly and the positioning falls apart. You need to actually have standards, even if that is just a minimum revenue threshold or a real commitment to being active.
2
Being vague about who qualifies. "This is for serious people only" says nothing. "This is for agency owners doing $10k+/mo who are done with tactics and ready to work on the business" is specific enough that people self-select accurately, and the right people feel called in.

The Open Scaffold
Free Lead Gen Free
built for scale, not for sale
The core idea: Genuine free value at scale is your growth engine. You become the go-to free resource in your niche first. Revenue comes from a paid product that lives elsewhere or is unlocked inside later.
Fits your community if: You have or are building a paid offer and want a warm audience. Also works if you monetise through sponsorships, services, or a back-end product rather than a monthly subscription.
Builder advantage: Free communities appear prominently in Skool Discovery. Your about page becomes organic SEO. The more specific and generous your description, the more people it pulls in without paid ads.
Copy template — replace the brackets with your own words
Everything here is free. No credit card. No pitch. [Community Name] is the free home for [who this is for] who want to [desired outcome] without paying for it upfront. What you get on day one: - [Free course or training resource] - [Free templates, tools, or downloads] - [Live events or community challenges] - [Number]+ members sharing what is working right now No ads. No upsells cluttering your feed. Just the best free resource for [niche] online. Join free in 30 seconds. If you ever want deeper support, there is a paid option too. But most people get everything they need for free.
Common mistakes with this blueprint
1
Saying it is free without explaining why. People are suspicious of free things. Tell them upfront what the model is: "This is free because I have a paid programme for people who want to go deeper. You will never need it to get value here." Transparency converts better than silence.
2
Not making the free value feel substantial. "Join for free and get access to our community" is weak. Name the specific things they get on day one: the course, the templates, the live event. Make it feel like they would be crazy not to join.

The Foundation Story
Personal Connection $9 to $67/mo
Then
No clear path forward
Trying everything alone
Wasting time and money
Now
A proven blueprint
A crew behind you
A faster result
The core idea: Sharing the exact struggle your audience is currently in creates instant recognition. When they see their own situation in your story, joining feels less like a purchase and more like a natural next step.
Fits your community if: You genuinely overcame the same challenge your audience faces today. The more precisely your past struggle matches their current one, the stronger the connection.
Builder advantage: Skool's about page supports longer text. This is the one format that rewards a fuller narrative. A 200-word origin story outperforms a 30-word tagline when the story is real.
Copy template — replace the brackets with your own words
A few years ago I was exactly where you are right now. [Describe their current situation in specific, honest terms] I tried [what they have already tried]. It did not work because [the real reason]. I wasted [time or money or energy] figuring that out. Then [the turning point: what you discovered or changed]. Today [the result you now have]. And I built this community so you get there in [timeframe] instead of [how long it took you]. Inside [Community Name]: - [What they will learn or access] - [The shortcut your journey revealed] - [The support structure you wish you had] [Price]/mo. Your story does not have to take as long as mine did.
Common mistakes with this blueprint
1
Making the story about you instead of them. The purpose of your story is to make the reader feel understood, not to impress them with your journey. Every sentence should land with the reader thinking "that is exactly what I am going through." The moment it becomes self-indulgent, you lose them.
2
Skipping the turning point. The structure is: struggle, turning point, result. Most people write the struggle and the result but rush or skip the turning point. That is actually the most important part, because it is the proof that the transformation is real and repeatable.

The Wrecking Ball
Problem First $9 to $49/mo
What they have
Overwhelmed
Spinning wheels
No traction
What they get
Clarity Momentum Results
The core idea: Name their frustration so precisely that they feel seen before you say a single word about your offer. When someone reads their own situation in your opening line, they are already half-converted.
Fits your community if: Your audience is stuck and knows it. Works best when you can articulate their problem in sharper language than they use themselves. Specificity is everything here.
Builder advantage: A sharp, problem-specific headline dramatically improves your visibility in Skool Discovery. Generic descriptions get scrolled past. Specific pain language stops people cold.
Copy template — replace the brackets with your own words
Still [specific frustration in plain language]? You have probably already tried [what they have already attempted]. You know the theory. The problem is not knowledge, it is [the actual bottleneck]. [Community Name] is built specifically for [qualifier] who are done with [the old broken approach] and ready to [the outcome they actually want]. Here is what shifts when you join: No more [pain point one] No more [pain point two] Instead you get [positive outcome one] And [positive outcome two] [Price]/mo. [X] people already made the switch. If that sounds like where you are, you are in the right place.
Common mistakes with this blueprint
1
Using generic pain language that applies to everyone. "Are you struggling to grow your business?" is not a Wrecking Ball. The power of this format is hyper-specificity. "Still rewriting your about page for the fifth time and getting zero signups?" hits a completely different nerve.
2
Staying in the problem too long. Two or three sentences of pain is a hook. Six sentences of pain is exhausting and starts to feel like the reader is being lectured about how bad their life is. Name it fast, acknowledge it clearly, then pivot to the solution.

The Off-Blueprint Build
Stand Out $5 to $35/mo
Copy the industry blueprint Follow the loudest voices Build like everyone else
Build it your way.
The core idea: Take a direct stance against the dominant way things are done in your niche. Name what is broken about the standard approach and position your community as the alternative people have been looking for.
Fits your community if: Your niche is full of noise, outdated advice, or over-complicated frameworks. Works when your audience has tried the popular approach and is frustrated that it has not worked for them.
Builder advantage: A bold contrarian about page gets shared. People screenshot it, post it, and tag others who feel the same way. Your about page becomes your first piece of organic content.
Copy template — replace the brackets with your own words
Ignore everything [the industry / the gurus / the standard playbook] told you about [niche topic]. Most [niche] advice is [the problem: overcomplicated / stale / built to sell courses not get results]. You have felt it. You follow the steps. Nothing moves. [Community Name] runs on one principle: [your contrarian belief stated plainly]. We do not [the thing everyone else does]. We [your different approach] instead. The results speak for themselves: [specific proof point or member win]. Inside: [what they get, stated simply] [Price]/mo. No fluff. Join [X] people who got tired of building from someone else's blueprint.
Common mistakes with this blueprint
1
Being contrarian without having a real alternative. Saying "forget everything you have been taught" only works if you follow it with a genuinely different approach. If your method is basically the same as everyone else's, the positioning falls apart the moment someone joins and looks around.
2
Coming across as angry rather than confident. There is a fine line between bold positioning and sounding bitter about the industry. The best version of this format is calm, clear, and slightly amused by how wrong everyone else has it, not ranting. Tone makes or breaks it.
